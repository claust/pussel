1. The pipeline today

Adding a puzzle runs through three independent stages in two processes. Only the last one does anything about perspective, and it does so with the least information of the three.

The one-line summary All five shots are collapsed onto the center shot’s viewpoint, which is itself an oblique handheld view. Rectification is attempted exactly once, at the very end, on the backend, from pixels alone — after every bit of camera geometry the phone knew has been thrown away.

2. Stage 1 — guided five-shot capture

GlareFreeCaptureController.steps defines five shots: a manual Center shot, then four automatic corner shots whose anchors sit at unit positions (0.25, 0.32), (0.75, 0.32), (0.75, 0.68), (0.25, 0.68) of the center shot. The user steers a guide dot pinned to that spot on the puzzle into a center ring; a dwell timer (GlareAimStabilityTracker) fires the shutter.

The guide dot comes from one of two sources behind the GlareGuideSource protocol:

  • Device — ARGlareGuideSource. An ARKit world-tracking session raycasts the four screen corners onto the detected horizontal plane. When all four hit, beginGuiding freezes that world-space quad as the puzzle outline and interpolates the step anchors into world targets on the plane. Every frame it projects outline and targets back into view points.
  • Simulator / E2E — GlareGuideTracker. Vision image registration against the reference shot. Translational results are unreliable on the Simulator and are never trusted for anything but keeping the E2E path alive.
Note what ARKit already knows and then discards A world-space quad on the table plane, the plane’s normal, and the full camera pose (simd_float4x4) at each of the five shutter moments. None of it is attached to the captured UIImages. captureShot() appends a bare image; the composer and the backend never see any of it.

3. Stage 2 — registration and masked healing

GlareFreeComposer.compose(reference:others:expectedShifts:) fuses the burst. In outline:

  1. Every frame is EXIF-uprighted and downscaled to a 2048 px long side (workingMaxDimension); registration runs on 1024 px proxies.
  2. Proxies are brightness-capped to flat gray so glare carries no gradient for the intensity-based aligner to chase, then blurred coarse and fine.
  3. Each corner frame is registered onto the center frame with VNHomographicImageRegistrationRequest, seeded (best first) by a translational bootstrap, the flow’s geometric expectedShift, and identity. Refinement iterates up to six passes; a converged warp must additionally pass a direct image comparison (alignmentError < 0.04) or the frame is dropped.
  4. Verified frames are warped with CIPerspectiveTransform and cropped to the reference’s extent — unfilled outside their own footprint.
  5. healedComposite applies per-frame photometric gain compensation, builds a robust vote-of-covering-frames darkening map, thresholds/dilates/feathers it into a glare mask, and blends the min-composite in only where the mask fires.
The geometric consequence Registration is relative. Each homography maps a corner shot onto the center shot; the composite therefore inherits the center shot’s perspective exactly. Whatever tilt the phone had when the user pressed the shutter is baked into the output, and the four corner shots — taken from four different, known-ish viewpoints — contribute nothing toward removing it. The composite is the correct output for a glare-removal algorithm and the wrong starting point for a fidelity-first overview.

4. Stage 3 — frame detection and warp

The composite is JPEG-normalized and POSTed to /api/v1/puzzle/detect-frame (backend/app/main.py), which calls PuzzleFrameDetector.detect_corners and then warp.

Detection — three layers, first hit wins

LayerHow it worksDesigned for
Color residual mask
_foreground_mask + _quad_from_mask		 Samples a 5 % border ring for a background chroma, measures ‖pixel − bg_chroma × luminance‖ (shadow-invariant), adds a brighter-than-background rule, morphologically cleans, fits a quad to the convex hull of the largest region. Bails when the border ring’s 99th-percentile residual exceeds 30 — i.e. when there is no uniform background. Puzzle on a plain floor/table with margin around it.
GrabCut recovery
_grabcut_quad		 When the mask has real evidence but no single region ≥ 15 % of the frame, seeds GrabCut with the surviving components as probable foreground and the border ring as definite background, then quad-fits the convex hull of what grows. Washed-out puzzles that fragment under warm light.
Edge quad
_edge_quad		 Auto-Canny → dilate → contours → first convex 4-gon from approxPolyDP (raw contour, then convex hull). Scored by rectangularity × edge-support-above-chance × area-ratio. Handheld shots where the puzzle nearly fills the frame — this is the path our five-shot composites usually land on.

No layer produces a quad → full-image corners with confidence 0.0.

The warp

warp orders the corners TL/TR/BR/BL by the sum/diff heuristic and maps them onto a plain axis-aligned rectangle whose size comes from the quad’s own edge lengths:

  • dst_w = max(‖TL→TR‖, ‖BL→BR‖)
  • dst_h = max(‖TL→BL‖, ‖TR→BR‖)

Then cv2.getPerspectiveTransform + cv2.warpPerspective at full resolution. Confidence and corners come back to the app; ConfirmTrimView shows the crop, flags anything under 0.4 as “Detection looks uncertain”, and offers rotation in 90° steps (the only geometric control the user has — there is no manual corner-drag on iOS, though the endpoint accepts a corners override that the web client uses).

5. Why the result is still skewed

The device screenshot that prompted this document shows the Ravensburger box crop leaning noticeably, with detection confidence 9 %. Three separate defects stack up.

Defect 1 — the quad is wrong fixed, see §7B

A five-shot composite of a box lying on carpet is exactly the case the color-residual path bails on (textured, non-uniform border) and the case the edge path is weakest at: a carpet has a high baseline edge density, so support_above_chance collapses and every candidate scores near zero. The winner is then whichever near-zero candidate happened to score highest — often a quad that includes carpet, or clips the box corner. A 9 % confidence is the detector correctly telling us it did not find the box; the crop we then show is a warp of the wrong four points, which is why it still looks tilted.

What it actually was, once measured The detected quad's top edge, right edge and top-left corner were correct. Its bottom-left corner sat ~90 px out into the box's own cast shadow — the contour trace had wrapped box and shadow into one region. Directional light gives that shadow a boundary as long and as straight as the box's real edge, so no amount of edge evidence separates them; only the direction of the colour step across the side does.

Defect 2 — the aspect ratio is invented

Described in the figure above. Even a correct quad yields a crop that is systematically squashed along the tilt axis. For a solver that matches pieces by grid cell, a wrong aspect ratio is a silent, uniform error in every cell coordinate.

Defect 3 — resolution is spent on the wrong thing

Detection is the last stage, so all five shots are resampled twice before it happens: once to 2048 px in the composer, once again by warpPerspective. The corner shots that are most foreshortened contribute their stretched pixels to the reference’s viewpoint rather than their sharp ones to a common rectified plane. Any super-resolution the five-view burst could offer is lost.

7. Improvement options

The user’s framing — “rectify each frame before stitching, or rectify the final image” — maps onto options A and C. They are not mutually exclusive; A is the structural fix, C is the cheap one, and B is the safety net that makes either trustworthy.

A · Rectify into a common plane done, device-measured

ARGlareGuideSource.captureStill() now returns a GlareFreeShot — the photo plus a PlaneCaptureGeometry (camera transform, intrinsics restated for the upright still, and the surface height frozen by the reference shot’s raycast). ARKit computed all of this already and the flow used to discard it. PlaneRectification turns it into geometry the composer can use, and the geometry is dumped with each burst so an offline re-run can tell a rectified capture from an unrectified one.

It is used in two places, and where turned out to matter more than the math:

  • Squaring up the composite — one warp of the finished image, after registration and healing.
  • Seeding registration — the two shots’ poses give the exact warp between them for anything on the table, offered as the first seed hypothesis.

The obvious design is wrong. Warping every frame onto the plane before registration is the natural reading of “rectify each frame, then stitch”, and it was tried first. ARKit reports the plane of the table, but the box stands centimetres above it and the five shots are taken from five different spots — so each frame’s subject is left displaced by its own parallax, the burst disagrees precisely on the thing being photographed, and healing fuses the disagreement. Measured on a real capture: lettering doubled, carpet smeared, while the same geometry applied to a single frame was pin sharp.

Vision’s registration, left alone, finds the plane the content lies on, which is the right one. Rectifying afterwards costs nothing: two parallel horizontal planes induce rectifying homographies that differ only by a uniform scale and translation, so squaring up the table squares up the box’s face too.

Do not fill what the camera did not see. Rectifying an oblique frame turns its rectangular footprint into a keystone, leaving wedges along the output edges. Filling them white — on the reasoning that a white edge is weaker evidence than a black one — put a long, straight, frame-spanning, high-contrast boundary into the composite, which is exactly what the lines generator hunts for. The detector preferred the fill boundary to the puzzle. The composite is now cropped to the largest axis-aligned rectangle the camera genuinely covered (PlaneRectification.coveredRect), so every pixel is real and the strongest rectangle left is the puzzle’s. On the capture that exposed this, confidence went 0.40 → 0.76 and residual skew 6.87° → 0.00°.

That last row was a side effect, not the goal. The plane seed beat both existing hypotheses on 28 of 28 corner frames across seven bursts, cutting the starting residual from ~0.30 to 0.05–0.24. Two things it revealed: the guided flow’s expected shift seed scores no better than identity — it models a hand-held reposition as a flat translation, which is not what that movement is — and the burst had been silently degrading to a single shot (no glare removal at all) more than half the time. It stays a seed: the box’s face is off-plane, so refinement still corrects it and the verification gate still decides. A bad seed costs passes, never a bad frame.

Still open:

  • Higher resolution is unlocked, not built. The frames are now genuine multi-view samples of one plane, which is the precondition for rendering above single-shot resolution; the composite is still rendered at the reference’s scale. Defect 3 stands.
  • Confidence is miscalibrated on soft-edged subjects. Poster captures score 0.34–0.44 with visibly correct quads, tripping the app’s “Detection looks uncertain” warning at 0.4. A false alarm, on a threshold tuned before any of this.
  • Not on the Simulator. No world tracking, so geometry is nil, and the composer registers and composites the frames as shot. The E2E path is unchanged, as is any device burst that never found the surface.
  • A little resolution at high tilt. The covered-region crop discards the keystone’s wedges, so an oblique shot yields a smaller output. Negligible at the 1–2° tilts now measured.

B · Detector fusion done

Detection was a fallback chain: the first layer that answered, won. It had no way to tell a confident wrong answer from a tentative right one. It is now a fusion — four generators always run, and their candidates compete on one score.

PieceWhat it does
lines generator new Groups long Hough segments into two roughly-orthogonal families, merges duplicates, and intersects pairs into quads. Needs no unbroken contour, so it survives the textured backgrounds that defeat contour tracing — and it wins on most real captures.
Unified scoring Rectangularity (squared), coverage, per-side edge support above chance, and border contrast, on one scale for every generator.
Border contrast new The term that fixed the screenshot. A real object differs from its surroundings in one colour direction on all four sides; a quad that has swallowed a cast shadow steps toward shadow on one side and toward bright cardboard on the others. Measured per sample as the fraction of a side behaving like a boundary, which also catches quads that clip a corner.
Confidence Now the boundary evidence only. Coverage still ranks candidates but is no longer reported: a pixel-perfect detection of a puzzle filling a fifth of the frame was reading as “uncertain”, which is a statement about framing, not about the detection.

Still open under B:

  • Detect on each of the five raw shots and fuse across them: the same box seen from five viewpoints gives five quads that must agree once mapped through the known homographies. Agreement would be a far better confidence signal than any single-image heuristic — and disagreement a reliable “ask the user” trigger. Needs the app to send more than the composite.
  • Run VNDetectRectanglesRequest on device as an additional candidate.
  • The manual corner-drag UI the API already supports (deferred by choice).

C · Rectify the final composite properly cheap, partial

  • Keep the current architecture; replace the max-edge-length destination rectangle with an aspect ratio estimated from the homography and the camera’s known focal length (the standard two-vanishing-point / IAC constraint), or snapped to the barcode motif’s aspect ratio when a lookup succeeded.
  • Straightforward, testable against scripts/stitch_quality/, and it fixes defect 2 for the Simulator path too — but it cannot fix defect 1 or 3, so a bad quad still yields a bad crop.

Suggested order

  1. Measure first. done scripts/rectify_quality/ runs the shipped detection and warp over a capture dump and reports, per image: which detector layer answered, the confidence, the quad’s IoU and corner error against a hand-labelled truth, the rectangle’s true aspect ratio recovered from the image quad, the aspect the crop actually got, the shot’s tilt, and the residual skew still left inside the crop. Its own self-tests validate each metric against a rectangle of known aspect rendered through a known camera. See that directory’s README.md.
  2. B, detector fusion. done Mean corner error nearly halved (54.3 → 30.2 px); the capture behind the screenshot went from 54 px to 8 px and now crops square-on. Manual corners deferred by choice — getting the rectangle right mattered more than a recovery path for getting it wrong.
  3. A, plane rectification. done The structural fix, and measured on device: residual skew 0.00° on every box capture, aspect error under 1%, and — unplanned — corner-frame registration up from 41% to 94%, so the burst finally removes glare instead of quietly falling back to one shot. The higher-than-single-shot resolution it unlocks is still unbuilt.
  4. C as the fallback aspect-ratio rule wherever A’s metric geometry is unavailable.
