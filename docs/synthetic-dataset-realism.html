Synthetic Dataset Realism — Findings & Test Plan

Why every model trained on synthetic puzzle data collapses on real photos (exp25–27), what the synthetic data actually gets wrong — measured, not guessed — and a ranked list of experiments to fix it. Based on a code audit of the generator, a quantitative real-vs-synthetic comparison on north_star data, and a literature review of sim-to-real techniques.

1 · Where we stand

Production uses the classical SIFT→NCC hybrid. The learned models tell a consistent story across three experiments:

The exp27 pair is the key diagnostic: the features transfer; any parameters trained on synthetic data become the contaminant. Non-learned readouts survive the camera (SIFT 76.7%, cosine 49.2%); every synthetic-trained readout collapses, and input-level domain randomization (exp26) does not save it.

Update 2026-07-26 — Test 1 ran, and the shortcut theory does not carry the collapse. exp30 fixed both label-leaking bugs (§4.1, §4.2) and retrained the exp26 recipe unchanged. The fix verifiably worked at the data level: the acceptance probes go from fail to pass (border touch 1.000→0.000 for 0°/180°, sharpness ratio 1.07→1.0000) and exp26's all-90°/270° rotation bias is gone — predictions are now near-uniform (990/902/989/895 over 0/90/180/270, uniform = 944).

But real transfer did not follow: north_star rotation rose only 33.5% → 37.9% against a 25% chance floor, cell accuracy did not move (22.3% → 21.5%), and both-correct went 12.7% → 13.2%. Synthetic accuracy meanwhile rose to a new learned best. Removing label leakage is necessary data hygiene worth ~4 points, not the cause of the sim-to-real collapse. Read §4.1 and §4.2 below as confirmed bugs that had to be fixed, not as the explanation — and weight §4.3 and §10 accordingly.

2 · The production framing: overview = box photo

In production the user photographs the puzzle box — the printed motif on glossy cardboard — and every piece's position and rotation is predicted against that box image. The overview is normally not a photo of the assembled puzzle. (north_star mixes the two: its protocol says “box poster or assembled puzzle”.)

This has three consequences for realism work:

  • Seam simulation is secondary. Rendering piece seams into the synthetic overview only matters for the assembled-puzzle capture mode and for part of north_star — it is not the production-critical gap.
  • The overview realism target is “print-and-photograph of box art”: a glossy printed surface under real lighting (specular glare), mildly perspective-corrected by the iOS pipeline, through a phone ISP, at ~100–150 px per puzzle cell.
  • Piece and overview are two different physical prints. The box art and the puzzle print are separate print runs — potentially different scale, tone curve, gamut, even slightly different crops of the motif (the backend's Ravensburger client already distinguishes clean motif vs box art). The synthetic pipeline models them as the same digital file, which is the deepest structural error (§4.3).

3 · The verdict: shortcuts, not realism

The sim-to-real collapse is mostly not a rendering-fidelity problem. The synthetic data contains cues that are near-perfectly predictive of the labels and structurally impossible in real photos — textbook shortcut learning (Geirhos et al., 2020). Any trainable parameter finds these cues and rides them; frozen features plus a non-learned readout cannot, which is exactly the exp27 split. Two of the cues are outright label-leaking generator bugs; a third — piece↔overview pixel identity — survives all of exp26's augmentations.

Consequence: “more realistic rendering” (Blender, gloss, embossed relief) is the last lever to pull, not the first. The stereo-matching literature hit this identical failure (synthetic pairs with pixel-identical local statistics) and fixed it with cheap data-side changes — a 7× error reduction from two augmentations (Chuah et al., 2021).

Revised after exp30: two of the three cues are now removed and real transfer is essentially unchanged, so “shortcuts” cannot be the whole story. The surviving candidates split into two families, and they make different predictions: either the remaining pixel-identity shortcut (§4.3) is doing all the work — testable by Test 2, which drives the NCC headroom down and retrains — or synthetic training data is the wrong instrument for this task at any realism level, as exp27 suggests, in which case the answer is §10's non-synthetic routes and further realism work has no ceiling worth reaching. Test 2 is worth running because it is the cheapest experiment that distinguishes these two, not because a fix is expected.

4 · The smoking guns

4.1 · A 100%-precision rotation shortcut generator bug

Synthetic pieces are cropped tight to the piece silhouette (cut_piece crops to getbbox(), shared/puzzle_shapes/image_masking.py:259-263) and rotated with expand=False at generation and load time. Verified on a 128×188 canvas:

Base rotationContent extent on canvas
0° / 180°Fills the canvas — alpha touches all four borders
90° / 270°Clipped (~60 px of tabs lost) with black bands top/bottom

So “piece touches all four borders ⟹ rotation ∈ {0°, 180°}” is a 100%-precision rule in synthetic train and test data. Real crops are pad_to_square'd with an 8% margin and never touch the borders — at test time the model is forced down the odd-rotation branch. This exactly reproduces exp26's diagnostic (“predicts 90°/270° for everything”) and explains 99.0% synthetic rotation vs 33.5% real. As a side effect the clipping destroys ~30–50% of the tab silhouette on half the training pieces — the one honest rotation cue.

4.2 · A rotation-correlated blur cue generator bug

rotate_rgba(expand=False, BILINEAR) on non-square canvases (116 of 120 pieces) puts 90°/270° rotations on half-pixel offsets. Measured on the same piece at all four rotations: mean gradient 5.01 / 4.56 / 5.01 / 4.56; |Laplacian| 9.90 / 6.68 / 9.90 / 6.68 — 90°/270° are 32% blurrier while 0°/180° are pixel-exact. Sharpness alone leaks the rotation label, only in synthetic data. Consistent with exp20's rotation accuracy (94.6%) far exceeding its cell accuracy (72.9%).

4.3 · The pixel-identity shortcut survives exp26 structural

The synthetic “overview” is the byte-identical source JPEG the pieces were cut from (exp20_realistic_pieces/dataset.py:182) — shared demosaic, white balance, JPEG block grid, noise fingerprint, sharpening. Measured masked zero-mean NCC of each piece against its ground-truth overview location (identical 7-scale × 7-rotation search per domain, decoy = same search on a random wrong cell):

DomainGT median NCC> 0.8Decoy median
Synthetic raw (exp20)0.990 (0°/180° exactly 1.000)56%0.471
Synthetic + full exp26 augmentation0.93784%0.528
Real (north_star)0.73041%0.407

exp26's photometric jitter is a near-affine intensity map that any contrast-normalized matcher cancels; its geometric jitter is absorbed by any scale/rotation search. The stated purpose of exp26 — breaking the pixel-exact shortcut — was not achieved at the correlation level. In production terms: the real box photo and the real piece photo are two independent captures of two different physical prints, with zero pixel identity. The synthetic pair is one file.

6 · What is ruled out

exp26 already randomized these, measured per-condition, and they transferred nothing — do not spend more effort here:

  • Backgrounds — all four capture backgrounds score the same (10–14% both).
  • Photometrics, piece-level sensor noise, JPEG — covered by exp26's ranges; zero transfer.
  • Grid size / puzzle aspect — the 4×4-only north_star subset scores the same as the full set (11.5% vs 12.7%).
  • Small geometric jitter (±8° rotation, mild perspective, ±15% scale) — present in exp26.

7 · Ranked test plan

Test 1 — Fix the two generator bugs DONE (exp30) · fix confirmed, hypothesis not

  • Square-pad synthetic pieces with the same 8% margin as the real path (pad_to_square), so no rotation ever touches the canvas border.
  • Use expand=True everywhere, and lossless np.rot90 for the 90° family, killing the half-pixel blur cue.
  • Pad-to-square before the 128×128 resize — training currently squashes aspect while deployment pads; they must agree.

Then retrain the exp26 model unchanged. Prediction was: north_star rotation jumps well above 33.5% and the 90°/270° bias disappears.

Result (exp30, 2026-07-26): half the prediction held. Implemented as lossless Image.transpose rotations plus exp24's 8%-margin pad_to_square framing applied at load time, exp26 recipe otherwise untouched (network/experiments/exp30_generator_fixes/).

The bias did disappear and the probes do pass — but rotation accuracy moved only 33.5% → 37.9% (chance = 25%), cell accuracy did not improve, and both-correct went 12.7% → 13.2% against the 76.7% bar. Synthetic test rose to 78.5%, the best learned result on this benchmark, which is precisely the exp26 pattern: synthetic gains, no transfer. The two bugs were real and are fixed; they were not what was breaking real-photo transfer. Do the fix anyway — it is correct, cheap, and removes a confound from every future measurement — but expect no rescue from it.

Test 2 — Break piece↔overview pixel identity structurally 1–2 days

Model the production truth: two independent captures of two different physical prints. Piece and overview must never share a pipeline instance:

  • Independent degradation chains per view — separate resample, blur/sharpen, noise, tone curve, and JPEG (independent block grid) for the piece and the overview. Never a shared pass.
  • Asymmetric Random Patching (stereo literature): with p=0.5, paste 2–4 random 50–100 px patches into one view only — the proven anti-pixel-identity augmentation (28%→4% error in synthetic→real stereo).
  • Resolution asymmetry matching reality: render the overview at the real per-cell pixel budget (~100–150 px/cell) and the piece from a high-res source (≥350 px native), instead of today's matched-MTF pair. Blocked on the corpus (measured 2026-07-26): the source images in network/datasets/puzzles are 256×256, i.e. 64 px per 4×4 cell — so a piece cannot be rendered at ≥350 px without inventing detail, and this is also the §5 6.5× high-frequency energy gap that no augmentation can close. Test 3's higher-resolution corpus is a prerequisite for this bullet, not an alternative to it. What is achievable on the current corpus is the asymmetry direction: give each view its own sampling lattice, resample kernel and subpixel phase so the pair no longer shares an MTF.
  • Run rembg on synthetic piece renders too, so both domains share the same segmentation artifacts (mask softness, tab-neck rounding) — the cheapest way to align edge statistics.
  • Box-photo overview realism: mild residual perspective (the iOS pipeline rectifies, imperfectly), specular glare blobs, lighting gradient, vignetting — Augraphy has ready-made transforms.
  • Optional, for the assembled-puzzle capture mode only: composite the overview from piece renders with 0.5–2 px seams and per-piece misalignment.

Test 3 — Swap the source corpus ~1 day · also unblocks Test 2

Train on cartoon/box-art-style motifs instead of (or mixed 50/50 with) Unsplash photos. The backend already has the Ravensburger motif CDN client (ravensburger.org/produktseiten/1024/{article}_1.jpg) — a few thousand real puzzle motifs at 1024 px are one script away, which also fixes the resolution gap. Keep north_star's 14 motifs out of training.

Test 4 — Fourier Domain Adaptation from a few real photos ~0.5 day

Swap the low-frequency amplitude spectrum of synthetic crops with spectra from ~20 unlabeled real photos (FDA, β ≤ 0.15, applied before normalization). Phase carries geometry, so cell/rotation labels are provably preserved. Needs no new capture — any real photos not in north_star work. Complements: histogram matching to real references, APR/FACT amplitude mixing.

Test 5 — Print + ISP simulation 2–3 days · after 1–4

  • Augraphy (pip, MIT): ink bleed / dot-matrix / letterpress / lighting-gradient / shadow-cast / reflected-light transforms.
  • ICC round-trip sRGB→CMYK→sRGB via PIL.ImageCms — reproduces gamut limiting in ~2 hours.
  • Halftone screening (halftone-converter: per-channel screen angles C15/M75/Y0/K45) with randomized frequency, angle, phase.
  • StegaStamp corruption ranges as a validated print-and-photograph starting config; ELD-style Poisson-Gaussian noise for the camera.

Rule from the face-anti-spoofing literature: randomize per-sample and apply independently to piece and overview — an identical simulated artifact in both views becomes the next shortcut.

In depth (Test 5 continued) — build order for a print-and-photograph chain

A dedicated deep-dive found no off-the-shelf “print then photograph” library exists — but the pieces are all portable. Recommended build order if/when Test 5 is warranted:

#StepDaysNotes
0 Measure first: px-per-cm of a piece in real photos; FFT a flat region of a real piece for the halftone and linen-emboss frequencies 0.5 Decides whether real screening is needed or a blur + tone curve suffices. Don't skip.
1 Port StegaStamp's transform_net (perspective, motion/defocus blur, hue, desaturation, brightness/contrast, noise, JPEG) with its ramp-in curriculum; use Diff-JPEG (BSD-3) for the JPEG stage 1–2 Trained against real printed-and-photographed images; the curriculum (distortions ramp from zero) matters as much as the ranges.
2 Add PIMoG-style light gradient/radial falloff + moiré blend 1 Repo is GPL-3.0 — reimplement from the published equations, don't vendor.

Validate against GEGL's newsprint.

No published spatial spec exists — measure it from a macro shot (step 0).
7 Sensor + ISP: port the Unprocessing shot/read-noise fit (log-linear read-vs-shot relation), Planckian white-balance jitter, and — underrated — ISP oversharpening (RingingOvershoot/UnsharpMask): the iPhone ISP sharpens aggressively 1.5 Real sensor statistics beat Gaussian noise by ~3 dB in the denoising literature.

Total ~7–11 days for the full chain — which is why Tests 1–4 come first. Augraphy's dev branch covers several camera-side pieces off-the-shelf (ReflectedLight glare, LightingGradient, LowLightNoise, Moire) but is CPU-only and document-oriented — pre-render offline and cherry-pick.

Two physical gaps the print literature does not cover, both flagged as likely bigger than any of the above:

(a) The piece is a 3D object, not a flat page. ~2 mm die-cut cardboard has an exposed grey core at the cut edge, a slightly domed face, and a cast shadow; the synthetic crop has razor edges with the neighboring piece's pixels immediately adjacent. This matches our measured bright-rim gap (§5) — and only a 3D-render route models it fully, though rembg-on-synthetic plus a rim/shadow band gets partway cheaply.

(b) Crop-mask jitter. Classical print-scan analysis (Solanki et al.) found mild cropping is the distortion that hits all frequency bands and destroys exactly the low-energy content. Synthetic pieces are cropped on an exact lattice; real ones are segmented with pixel slop and background bleed. Modeling segmentation slop explicitly is nearly free and folds into Test 2.

Skip for now low expected value

  • Full PBR rendering (Blender/BlenderProc): 8–13 days; documented sim-to-real lifts are 10–35 points on tasks that were not at ~0% transfer, and exp27 says the features are not the problem. Revisit only if failure analysis specifically implicates the specular lobe or the die-cut edge.
  • CycleGAN-style translation: documented to hide steganographic cues in its output — silent label corruption on a task whose signal is fine texture.
  • Adversarial feature alignment (DANN/ADDA): unselectable without target labels; matched by plain ERM under fair tuning.
  • Spectral ink models (Yule-Nielsen-spectral-Neugebauer, Hersch ink spreading): no public code, needs a spectrophotometer and printed calibration charts, and the 1–2 ΔE it buys is swamped by unknown room lighting. The γ-per-ink tone curve captures the useful part.
  • Deep halftoning: the literature targets blue-noise dithering for display quality, not CMYK offset screening with rosettes — wrong artifact class entirely.

10 · Beyond synthetic realism

Realism fixes should remove the penalty of synthetic training. The cheapest routes to actually beating 76.7% may not be synthetic at all — worth holding in parallel:

  • A small fresh real training set. Literature says 12–100 real instances per class often suffice for readout fitting. 50–200 newly photographed pieces (a few hours' capture, never overlapping north_star) used to fit only the small readout on frozen DINOv2 — with LP-FT so the features stay undistorted — attacks exp27's finding head-on.
  • Distill from the classical matcher. The SIFT→NCC hybrid is a 76.7%-accurate teacher over unlimited unlabeled real photos. SuperPoint-style warp-ensembling (keep only cells where the ensemble agrees) gives confidence-filtered pseudo-labels for free.
  • The two-capture trick. Photograph any printed poster or the box itself twice — once wide (“overview”), once close (“piece” scale) — register by homography, then cut synthetic Bezier pieces from the close-up. Real print, real ISP, real resolution asymmetry, zero pixel identity, labels free from the homography. ~2–3 days including the photo session.
  • Test-time adaptation for whatever ships: recompute BN statistics from ~32 unlabeled real images (CNN), or closed-form CORAL / T3A prototypes on frozen DINOv2 features — no gradients, no synthetic contamination.
